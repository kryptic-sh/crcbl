what is actually running

A T-handle in zero gravity, flipping.

The orange handle is spun about its middle axis — the one whose moment of inertia sits between the other two — with a tiny nudge about another. That spin is unstable, and the handle turns itself over and back again for as long as the page is open. It is the Dzhanibekov effect, and nothing on this page drives it: it falls out of the inertia tensor the two boxes add up to and the gyroscopic term in the rotation equations.

The counters are the claim. A free body keeps its angular momentum and its energy, and a simulation that did not would show it here as a handle slowly settling into a steady spin. The integrator solves the gyroscopic step by the implicit midpoint rule, which conserves both, and the panel shows how far each has drifted, in trillionths.

A box dropped flat, and the floor it cannot land on.

The blue box is dropped with no spin, and it gains none: its angular velocity stays exactly zero and its orientation stays exactly level all the way down. Then it falls straight through the floor. That is not a bug — there is no contact solver yet, and landing is what the next rung builds. The box is put back at the top each time and the panel counts the drops.

The hash is the same everywhere. Nothing in a physics step calls a platform's maths library: the operations it uses are ones every processor performs identically, and the sine and cosine it needs are built inside the engine from them. So the state hash this page reaches ten seconds in is the very number the native build's test pins, and the browser test checks the two agree.
